FUSE

Structured execution from empirical categories

FUSE explores an agent paradigm that learns reusable, scoped regularities from documents, interfaces, interactions, and counterexamples—then revalidates them locally before acting in a new environment.

Category agents beyond a fixed ontology

Categories are revised from operational consequences and future tasks, so the state distinctions, atomic actions, and relations remain tied to what an agent must actually do.

2

Empirical categories with scoped effects

Local experience retains provenance, applicability, conflict, and dependency. Reusable conclusions can compose across tasks, while uncertainty becomes a concrete observation or validation request.

3

Mathematical representation as a guide

Compositional representations expose missing observations and construction obligations. The longer-term aim is to turn those obligations into reliable plans for long-horizon agents and scientific workflows.

Multi-Agent Large Language Models for Connectivity Restoration in Damaged Graphs

Intent-aware structural repair for damaged graphs

A graph-repair workflow that grounds natural-language intent in the damaged topology, explicit edge constraints, and transparent repair objectives before selecting a strategy.

Explore the project
Public boundary The manuscript and a compact, safety-hardened core are public. Provider code, private prompts, datasets, and the full experiment harness are omitted.
1

Graph structure as shared evidence

Connected components, positions, forbidden edges, and required connections define the repair state; decisions are grounded in these objects rather than inferred from prose alone.

2

Intent becomes explicit objectives

Preferences such as low cost or low latency are translated into inspectable priorities over connectivity, added edge length, path efficiency, and robustness.

3

Plans are selected and verified on the graph

Transparent bridge, component-tree, latency, and compositional candidates are compared on graph copies; the selected edit plan is checked again against connectivity and hard constraints.

Theory2Method

Partial differential equations (PDEs) underpin models of transport, diffusion, fluids, elasticity, and many other physical systems, while AI4PDE increasingly seeks reusable learned solvers for parameterized PDE families. Most neural PDE pipelines, however, select a representation or model architecture before determining whether mathematical theory can fundamentally restructure the computational problem. We introduce Theory2Method, a multi-agent system that uses mathematical theory upstream of solver selection. All tasks start from the same task-independent taxonomy of broad mathematical theory categories and a common original-source search environment. A Theory Search Agent retrieves candidate mathematical results and selects theories whose assumptions match the target PDE and whose conclusions have plausible computational relevance. A Method Design Agent then grounds the selected theory into solver-relevant computational structure, applicability conditions, and a typed unresolved computation. A deterministic type-aware selector uses this unresolved computation to choose a numerical or learned implementation route, while coupled validators assess both theory-instance validity and method effectiveness. Across a diverse PDE portfolio, the resulting methods include exact numerical transformations, structure-guided learned solvers, and rejection of mathematically inadmissible proposals. The retained learned methods improve their prespecified matched comparators while satisfying the corresponding theory-derived validity conditions. These results demonstrate a practical role for large language model agents in AI4PDE beyond architecture or hyperparameter search: using mathematical theory to determine the representation, prediction target, and computational allocation before model training.

Only this abstract is public. The manuscript remains private while under review.
